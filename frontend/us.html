Research screens — reading a score

This is the US board's counterpart to India's "How this board is built" page, and it follows the same structure. Where the US version deliberately differs from India's, the row says so. Every threshold below is the one backend/scripts/us_auto_screen.py and us_lenses.py actually apply.

Claim grade

Claim gradeWhat it means
company-statedThe only grade the US screen produces. The company says it, in a filing, and nothing has contradicted it. An assertion in a 10-K is the strongest form of this, but it is still the company describing itself.
verifiedNot produced yet. It needs an independent third party (a regulator's database, a rating agency) and that is a research step, not something a keyword rule can grant.
none claimed / DEBUNKEDNot produced yet; both come from a human reading the claim against a primary source.

The five paths — any one is enough

Same rule as India: the point is to find a company that financials alone would never surface, so clearing one path is sufficient and the fundamentals gates never block it (they are still computed, and the card lists exactly which it fails). Two things every path still needs: an allowed sector, and a holder-of-record count under 25,000.

PathWhat it needs
1. Moat evidenceThe company describing itself, in its latest 10-K, as a leading or largest provider, holding a stated market share, the only or one of a limited number of providers, first or a pioneer, or in a niche segment that carries a regulatory approval (FDA, FAA, UL, ISO, NRC and similar). Reshoring / reduced dependence on Chinese suppliers replaces India's import substitution. Only sentences about the company itself count — not its suppliers, an acquired subsidiary, or an industry overview.
2. Management guidanceThe latest earnings release guides revenue growth above 15%. A stated percentage counts; so does a guided dollar range, converted against last fiscal year's revenue (marked derived). Guidance is read from the earnings release only, because US companies give it there and a 10-K's risk-factor boilerplate is misread by the shared patterns.
3. PAT turnaroundNet income in the latest quarter is positive and at least one of the previous three quarters was a loss, from SEC's XBRL filings. Q4 is never reported on its own, so it is derived as the fiscal year minus the first three quarters.
4. Capacity-utilization rampA sentence about the company saying utilization will rise, tied to a named near-term period. A capacity expansion alone does not count, and neither does "utilization should improve" with no timeframe.
5. Product-mix pivotA sentence about the company describing a strategic move (diversifying, entering a new segment, a new product line) explicitly tied to a shift in market demand. Routine capacity on the same product does not count.

Paths 2, 4 and 5 are text matches: the matched sentence travels with the flag, labelled Auto-detected, unverified, so you can read it before believing it.

The columns and badges

ColumnDefinition and how to read it
CWIP %NBConstruction in progress as a percentage of net property, plant and equipment, from the PP&E note in the 10-K. Money already spent on plant that isn't producing yet. Blank is normal in the US: most companies don't report a CIP line (2 of the 10 on the board today do), and SEC's structured feed leaves it out, so this reads the row from the filing text and leaves the field empty rather than guessing.
⚑ PE+CWIPP/E above 40 and CWIP at least 15% of net PP&E: the market is paying a rich multiple on earnings that exist while a real slice of the asset base isn't earning yet. It cuts both ways — growth genuinely ahead of earnings, or a price that has run ahead of a plant that isn't proven.
🏗 heavy capexA softer, valuation-blind version: CWIP at least 25% of net PP&E, whatever the multiple.
▲ guides >15%The latest earnings release guides revenue growth above 15%. The Guide % column carries the number. Where the release gave only a dollar range, the percentage is derived from it and last fiscal year's revenue and the card says so. A forward statement that refuses to quantify shows as a grey guidance badge with no number, and is found under "Any forward growth statement".
↻ PAT+Latest quarter profitable after a loss in one of the previous three. The drawer lists the last quarters so you can see what actually swung — some turnarounds are one-off gains rather than operating recovery.
⚙ capacity / ⇄ pivotPaths 4 and 5 above.
fails N gatesThe company cleared a path but not the fundamentals gates (market cap band, ROE above 5%, holders of record). Hover to see which. Added anyway by design; the score is not adjusted for it.

How the score is built

Before scoring, a company must clear the hard filters below. Survivors are scored on five pillars.

Hard filterThreshold
Size & obscurityMarket cap $30m–$2,000m, and fewer than 25,000 holders of record. The holder cap is required by every path (India's known to the public test). Holder counts run low in the US because most shares sit in street name, so it is a weaker signal here than India's shareholder count.
SectorFinance, banking, insurance, real estate, hotels, restaurants, media, entertainment, software, retail and utilities are skipped, the same list India uses, applied to Finnhub's industry.
Quality floor (recorded, not required)ROE above 5%. A company that clears a path but not this is still added and the failed gate is shown on its card.
Promoter, institutional, public floatNot applied. The US has no promoter concept, and institutional ownership (13F) isn't on Finnhub's free tier. Insider ownership is scored as a pillar instead.
PillarWeightWhat it measures
Moat258 points per strong statement (leading provider, stated share, sole provider, first mover), plus 4 for a qualified niche, plus 2–5 for return on investment as evidence the barrier is real.
Reshoring20The company describes reduced dependence on overseas suppliers or a domestic-supply-chain advantage. All or nothing; India's import-substitution pillar scores how concrete the claim is.
Insider15Insider (officer and director) ownership: 5–25% scores highest; above 25% less; known but under 5% low; unknown lowest. From Finnhub's insider filings, so it is an approximation.
Under-covered15A smaller market cap and fewer holders of record score higher: the names the crowd hasn't found.
Financials10ROE and return on investment.
Institutional—India scores 15 here. Not scored in the US (no free data), which is why the maximum is 85, not 100.
AdjustmentEffectWhy
Risk penalty−3 to −8Subtracted after scoring: fewer than 1,000 holders of record (−5), P/E above 60 (−3). Every penalty is itemised on the company's card. India's other penalties (pledge, Tier 2, red-flag count) have no US data behind them.
Confidence adjustmentnoneIndia scales a hand-researched record by how complete its data is. An auto-screen record has one fixed shape, so there is nothing to scale.

Every flag on this board is a rule matching a company's own words or SEC's numbers. Rules make mistakes — read the quoted sentence on the card, and treat a score as a reason to look, not a conclusion.
