Research screens — reading a score

This board merges work from separate runs that asked different questions. Scores are comparable within a rubric and misleading across one. Every row carries its screen and, where it matters, a v4 marker on the score.

ScreenNamesWhat it was looking for, and what its score means
v3 deep—Undiscovered quality. Full deep-dive workups. Gated on size (₹120–4,000 cr), fewer than 40,000 retail holders, promoter ≥40%, ROCE ≥12%, ROE >5%. Scored out of 100 on six pillars — moat 25, import substitution 20, promoter 15, institutional 15, under-covered 15, financials 10 — then confidence-adjusted and reduced by an itemised risk penalty.
v7 depth—Depth round, 20 Aug 2026. Half filled out themes that were too thin to be useful — medical devices, testing, water, healthcare, railways, marine, logistics, IT hardware. Half opened new ground: renewables and EV supply chain, cables, refractories, industrial gases, paper, ethanol, safety and fire, elevators and HVAC, tools and fasteners, appliances and lighting, minerals, glass and ceramics. Same gates, same rubric.
v3 screen—Same gates, same rubric, less work. These cleared every v3 gate and were scored, but never received the full deep-dive. The narrative on each is a first look, not a finished view — treat accordingly.
v4 moat—Defensibility, not obscurity. Deliberately not gated on size or shareholder count, because a sole domestic manufacturer of anything worthwhile usually gets found. Scored on a different 100-point rubric: exclusivity 25, certification depth 15, pricing power 25 (scored off the actual five-year operating-margin series, not the narrative), switching costs 15, returns quality 10, balance sheet and governance 10. A v4 score of 60 is not "worse" than a v3 score of 70 — it is measuring something else.
triage—Checked, never scored. Taken far enough to reject or park. They carry a verdict and one decisive fact each, and no score at all — they sort to the bottom because they are unranked, not because they ranked last.
v5 sweep—Fresh sweep of the core themes, 20 Aug 2026. Defence, railways, marine, electronics, chemicals, capital goods and energy hardware. Run on the v3 gates and the v3 rubric, with the three new lenses used as tie-breakers.
v6 sectors—Sector expansion, 20 Aug 2026. Nine themes the board had barely touched: packaging and printing, plastics, building materials, technical textiles, agri and food processing, logistics, water and environment, healthcare services, IT hardware. Same gates, same rubric. These sectors contain a lot of commodity converting and monsoon-dependent cyclicals, and the moat scores say so — several land in single digits by design.
v8 moat—Moat scoring wave, rubric v5. Adds a "strategic" pillar in place of "under-covered" — used for names (defence PSUs, large caps) where market obscurity isn't the point but strategic positioning is.
v8 weekly—Weekly refresh sweep. The recurring pass that keeps existing names current between the larger research rounds above.
🌊 Deep Sweep—Ocean-wave deep scan. A dedicated high-conviction pass designed to surface only the most compelling, quality-first names with strong structure and a cleaner moat profile.
added on request—Names you asked for by hand. Several fail the v3 gates outright; each card lists exactly which, and the score carries an explicit penalty for it.
Claim grade (v4 only)What it means
verifiedAn independent third party says it — an ICRA/CRISIL/CARE rating rationale, a regulator (FSSAI, RDSO, WOAH), or a customer-conferred award. This is the only grade that is evidence rather than assertion.
company-statedThe company says it and nothing contradicts it. An assertion in a statutory annual report is the strongest form of this, but it is still the company describing itself.
none claimedThe company makes no exclusivity claim at all. If a name reached the list on a monopoly framing, that framing came from somewhere other than the company.
DEBUNKEDA primary source contradicts the claim, or the company itself has quietly retreated from it. Several of these circulate widely in Indian investing commentary. They are kept on the board deliberately, so the work does not get repeated.

The single most useful finding from the v4 run: market share and pricing power are close to uncorrelated in this data. I G Petrochemicals holds ~50% of India's phthalic anhydride capacity on a 5–6% operating margin. Rajratan is ICRA-confirmed as the sole bead-wire supplier in Thailand and its margin fell from 19% to 13% while volumes grew 55%. Meanwhile Bhansali Engineering Polymers is not a monopoly at all and holds 15–19% margins through every raw-material cycle. Where share and pricing power do line up, it is usually a regulatory or qualification moat rather than a structural one.

The three new columns, and exactly what they mean

ColumnDefinition and how to read it
CWIP ₹cr and CWIP %NBCapital work-in-progress at the latest balance-sheet date, in rupees crore and as a percentage of net block (net fixed assets). CWIP is money already spent on plant that is not yet producing anything — it sits on the balance sheet earning nothing and depreciating nothing until commissioned. A rising CWIP balance is a growth signal; a large one relative to the existing asset base is also an execution risk, because the whole thesis depends on that plant starting up and filling.
⚑ PE+CWIPFlags a company where P/E is above 40 and CWIP is at least 15% of net block. This is the combination you asked for: the market is already paying a rich multiple on earnings that exist, while a material slice of the asset base has not started earning at all. It cuts both ways — it can mean the growth is genuinely ahead of the earnings, or that the price has run ahead of a plant that has not been proven yet.
🏗 heavy capexA softer, valuation-blind version: CWIP is at least 25% of net block, whatever the multiple. This catches the cheap capex stories the P/E filter misses.
▲ guides >15%Management has made a forward revenue-growth statement above 15%. The Guide % column carries the number. Where management gave a rupee target rather than a percentage, the percentage is derived from their own two figures and the card says so. Where they made a forward statement but refused to quantify it, the badge is grey and the number is blank — those are surfaced under "Any forward growth statement". An order book or a capacity expansion is not counted as guidance.
↻ PAT+Profit after tax in the most recent reported period is positive and at least one of the previous three periods was a loss. Note the period type: many SME names report half-yearly, so "the last three periods" can span eighteen months. Every turnaround card says what actually drove the swing — several of these are divestment gains or investment mark-to-market, not operating recoveries.
added on requestNames added at your request rather than surfaced by the screen. Several of them fail the screen's own hard gates on size, shareholder count or returns; each card lists exactly which gates it fails, and the score carries an explicit penalty for it.

How the score is built

Every name must first clear four hard filters. Survivors are then scored out of 100 across six pillars. The displayed score is confidence-adjusted — a company with thin data cannot outrank one with a complete record.

Hard filterThreshold
Promoter skin in the gamePromoter holding ≥ 40%; promoter pledge below 40% (above 10% flagged)
Institutional validationTier 1: FII + DII ≥ 1% — institutions already on the register. Tier 2: passes every other gate but has no meaningful institutional holding yet. Tier 2 exists because this filter was removing the most genuinely undiscovered names in the market; they carry a −3 score penalty and are labelled everywhere
Retail floatPublic holding ≤ 60%
Size & obscurityMarket cap ₹120 cr – ₹3,000 cr (Tier 2 up to ₹4,000 cr) and fewer than 25,000 retail shareholders (Tier 2: 40,000). This is the filter that removes the names you already know
Quality floorROCE ≥ 12% and ROE > 5% — a moat that does not show up in returns on capital is not a moat
PillarWeightWhat it measures
Moat25Specificity of the competitive barrier (approvals, qualification cycles, IP, switching costs) plus ROCE level and operating margin as evidence the barrier is real
Import substitution20How concrete and evidenced the import-replacement claim is — a named imported product and origin scores far higher than a generic "Make in India" framing — plus revenue growth as proof the substitution is actually happening
Promoter15Promoter holding level (peak reward 55–70%; very high stakes discounted for float risk), pledge penalty, and direction of the promoter stake over the last year
Institutional15Both FII and DII present scores highest; the sweet spot is 2–15% total — validated but not yet crowded. Above 25% is treated as already discovered
Under-covered / Strategic15Analyst coverage, market-cap band, and the retail shareholder count for most names; for the v5-rubric "strategic" pillar (large/PSU names), strategic positioning instead
Financials10ROE, debt/equity, three-year profit growth, Piotroski score
AdjustmentEffectWhy
Confidence adjustment×0.72–1.0Scales the pillar total by how complete the company's data record is, so a thinly-documented name cannot outrank a fully verified one on the strength of missing fields
Risk penalty−2 to −23Subtracted after scoring: verification caution (−4), import-substitution thesis rated weak (−5) or absent (−6), fewer than 1,500 shareholders (−7) or fewer than 3,000 (−5), P/E above 60 (−3), promoter pledge above 10% (−3), three or more red flags (−2), Tier 2 status (−3), and failing the screen's own hard gates (−4 per gate, capped at −8) for names added on request. Every penalty is itemised on the company's card

How this stays current

Every company name in the table is a live link to its screener.in page — the same source the numbers came from — so you can check any figure at its source in one click. The scorecard drawer adds direct links to filings and annual reports, the exchange quote page, and a news search.

The figures still come from a periodic data-refresh pass (screener.in / Trendlyne), not a live market feed — see scripts/refresh_data.py in the project for how to re-run it and re-seed the database.     ⚠ Caveats — data quality, guidance, one-off PAT gains, and liquidity
    Read this before acting on any of it. This is a research screen, not investment advice — I am not a registered investment adviser and none of this is a recommendation to buy or sell. Data was pulled from third-party aggregators and company filings and can be stale, mis-tagged, or simply wrong; verify every figure against the company's own filings before committing capital. On the new columns specifically: CWIP is reported inconsistently — several companies here capitalise spend directly without ever showing it as CWIP, so a zero does not always mean no capex, and where that is likely the card says so. Management guidance is a statement of intent by people with an interest in the share price, not a fact; some guidance entries are second-hand paraphrases rather than verified transcripts and are labelled as such, and several percentages are derived from a rupee target management gave rather than quoted. A PAT turnaround can be an operating recovery or a one-off gain. Read the card before treating any flag as a signal. A quantitative screen also cannot read management quality, related-party dealings, or accounting aggression. The smallest names here have as few as a few hundred retail shareholders; you may not be able to exit at a quoted price, or at all, on a bad day. Many are recent SME listings whose share prices have moved sharply on a short operating history. Treat this list as a research starting point, not a buy list.
